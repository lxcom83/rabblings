# Artwork needed for the new website

Every image the rebuilt site has a place for but does not have yet. Nothing on the site breaks without them: each one shows as a labelled empty plate (or, for the hero banner, the pixel-art backdrop stays) until the file exists.

## How to add a finished image

1. Save it in `site/img/journal/` with the exact filename below, extension included (convert to WebP if Gemini gives you PNG or JPEG; the pages look for the exact name).
2. Run `python3 build.py`. It lists the files in `docs/img/journal/list.json`, and the pages pick them up by themselves.
3. Keep each file under about 300 KB (WebP quality 80 is usually right). Portrait study pages at 1200 x 1500 px are plenty.

## How to use the prompts

- Every prompt below is complete: copy the whole block into a fresh Gemini chat.
- For journal pages (race studies, settlements, creatures, lore plates) attach the approved **Mushroom Folk study** to the chat and add the words *"match the drawing style, layout and clean paper of this page"*, as the Race Art Briefs doc asks, so every page looks drawn by the same hand.
- Gemini's handwriting does not need to be perfect. The website never relies on text inside an image; if you want the notes cleaned up, keep a version without annotations too.
- Check each result against the art brief's warnings before keeping it (real-world buildings, runes, skull piles, humans, pink).

**A note on paper.** The owner's art brief says the scholar's *original* pages are enchanted and stay clean and fresh, so the race studies, creatures, settlements and lore plates below ask for clean paper. The website itself is presented as a later *printed translation*, so the site's own pages look aged (that is all CSS), and the two images that belong to the translators' edition (hero banner, social card) ask for aged paper. If you would rather every image looked aged, swap the paper paragraph for the one in the hero banner prompt.

You are making concept art for Rabblings, a grimdark fantasy game series. Follow these rules in every image. Style: bold cartoon with thick near-black outlines (#1a1216), flat shading with one shadow tone and one highlight, slightly chunky proportions. Bleak and darkly funny: mud, rot, mist and dying light. The humour comes from the characters, never bright colours. Palette: muted and earthy. Do not copy real-world architecture (no pagodas, onion domes, minarets, Gothic cathedrals or temples from real cultures) and no real religious symbols (no crescents, crosses or similar). No domes. Writing: no letters, words, names, numbers or real alphabets anywhere, including Norse runes. Signs and banners carry simple invented symbols only. No glowing rune stones or carved glyphs, except Dwarf rune marks where the brief asks for them. Skulls: one skull on a post per settlement, plus a few scattered bones. No skull piles, skull rows or skull chains, except where the brief asks for them. Technology: weapons are blades, clubs, spears, bows and crude blackpowder at most. Small settlements have no industry. No rubber tyres, electricity or modern machinery. There are no humans in this world, so never draw one. Only show the race named in the brief. Do not add other peoples, chained or tethered floating islands, or details the brief does not mention. Avoid: pink and pastel colours, bright saturated colours, happy smiles, cute mascots, photorealism, heavy gore beyond old stains, guns, modern technology, and anything resembling existing games or characters.

This image is a page from the journal of a wandering scholar in the Rabblings world. It replaces the bold cartoon style for this image only. Style: highly detailed naturalist's field-journal page. Fine pen-and-ink with careful cross-hatching, and a light watercolour wash used sparingly only to record true colours. Every page looks drawn by the same steady, experienced hand. Paper: clean, fresh parchment. The journal is magically preserved, so no stains, tide marks, burns, tears, yellowing or ageing, not even small marks. Annotations: a few short handwritten notes in English, as if the page has already been translated, with thin leader lines pointing to details. Each note sits right beside the part it describes, and its leader line is short and ends exactly on that part: a note about the face points to the face, a note about the hands points to a hand. A leader line never crosses a wing, a body or another drawing to reach its note, and a note that does not clearly belong to one part is left off. Every word must be a real, correctly spelled word, written in a dry, matter-of-fact voice. No runes. Never write the descriptions from this prompt onto the page as titles or labels. No page numbers, dates, month names, units of measurement or headings. Never name other races or creatures (no orcs, elves or similar), and no real-world species names or Latin scientific names. Never repeat the same note twice. Include one small detail in the drawing that no annotation points to. No signature, monogram, initials, wax seal or signet. The scholar never appears, and there are no humans in this world. The tone is dry and observational: the subject is drawn as it really is, never cute or heroic. Faces are never pretty, sweet or friendly.

Layout: one large full-body study of the subject in front and side view, surrounded by smaller close-up studies, one or two small vignettes of behaviour, and a simple scale bar with tick marks but no numbers.

Non-human rule: this people must not read as a human in a costume. Take the traits listed under EXAGGERATE and push them well past human proportions so the silhouette is unmistakably its own species, while keeping one calm, readable body (weird, not overwhelming, never a horror-film monster). Do not add traits that are not listed. EXAGGERATE: ears longer than the head is tall, swept back; fingers almost twice human length; a narrow, hunched, bandy-legged frame about half the height of a dwarf; a wide lipless mouth of small uneven fangs; large flat yellow eyes with slit pupils.

Journal study of a goblin. Full figure in front and side view: sickly olive skin, long pointed ears, yellow eyes, a ragged dark red pointed hood and mismatched scavenged gear. Close-ups: the face and small fangs, long-fingered hands, a notched rusty blade, a pouch of scavenged odds and ends. Vignettes: two goblins quarrelling over loot, and a goblin crouched beside a hidden pit covered with sticks. Wash colours: olive skin, dark red hood, yellow eyes.

Output: a single portrait page, 4:5 aspect ratio, about 1200 x 1500 pixels. Keep everything important away from the outer 5% of the image, which may be cropped on phones.
